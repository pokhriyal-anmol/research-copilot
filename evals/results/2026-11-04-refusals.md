# Why the demo refused 26 of 120 answerable questions

Written 2026-11-04. The measurement plan below was recorded before any identifiability label came back.

## What the model was given

Retrieval was replayed for all 120 reviewed questions with the demo's own settings (hybrid search, 50 candidates per retriever, rerank pool 30, 8 passages sent). Search is deterministic, so this is what each question received on 2026-11-03.

| | Known passage sent | Same paper, other page sent | Known passage outside the 30-passage pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answered (94) | 79 | 10 | 5 |
| Refused (26) | 4 | 3 | 19 |

All 19 missing passages exist in the database (2 to 9 chunks on that page). In vector search alone, 12 of them are not in the top 500 and the other 7 rank between 32nd and 164th.

Reading the 19, most do not say which paper they are about, for example "How many A800 graphics processing units were used to run the experiments?". Over 299 papers such a question has many correct answers or none, so refusing can be right. The cause is in the question generator: its prompt said "Don't mention 'the passage' or 'this paper'", which removed the context that identifies the paper. Of the 4 refusals where the passage was sent, 2 are over-refusals by the model, 1 was a question left with a `<think>` block from the generator, and 1 is too vague to answer.

## Fixes

- `draft_questions.py` now strips `<think>` blocks and its prompt asks for a question that names its method, system, dataset or task.
- The one question with a `<think>` block was cleaned in `evals/questions.csv`.

## Re-measure, recorded before labelling

Two AI labellers, working separately and seeing only the question text, mark each of the 120 questions identifiable or not under the same rubric. A question counts as identifiable only if both say so; questions they split on are reported separately. On each group: refusal rate, and the share whose known passage was among the 8 sent. No new model calls; the 2026-11-03 answers are reused. If identifiable questions still show a refusal rate above 10%, retrieval is the next fix.

## Result

Labellers F and H agreed on 108 of 120 questions (Cohen's kappa 0.80). A third labeller, G, was discarded before its labels were used: it gave one generic reason to all 76 of its "not identifiable" labels.

| Group | Questions | Refused | Known passage among the 8 sent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identifiable (both labellers) | 44 | 2 (4.5%, 95% interval 1 to 15%) | 41 (93%) |
| Not identifiable (both labellers) | 64 | 23 (36%) | 31 (48%) |
| Labellers split | 12 | 1 | 11 |

On questions that say what they are about, the demo refuses 4.5%, under the 10% bar set above, so retrieval is not the next fix. The 22% came mostly from questions with no single right answer in a 299-paper index. The two identifiable refusals are real retrieval misses: neither known passage reached the 30-passage rerank pool.

Two things follow. Only 44 of the 120 reviewed questions identify their paper, so the eval set should be redrafted with the fixed generator before it is used for another before-and-after number. And real users will ask unidentifiable questions too; the product answer to those is to say which papers report it, or ask which paper is meant, not to refuse.
